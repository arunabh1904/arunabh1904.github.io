## 2022 – Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback

**arXiv:** [2212.08073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)

## Summary

> Constitutional AI replaces most harmfulness labels with an explicit list of principles and two model-mediated stages. The supervised stage produces critiques and revisions of harmful responses. The reinforcement stage asks a model to choose between responses under a sampled principle, trains a preference model, and optimizes the assistant against that model.

## Core Insights

Constitutional AI addresses expensive and opaque harmlessness supervision in two stages. A helpful RLHF model answers red-team prompts, critiques its own harmful responses under a sampled principle, and revises them. Those revisions train a supervised model. The same constitution then directs a feedback model to compare sampled responses, producing the preference data for RLAIF. Human labels for helpfulness remain in the setup; the harmfulness comparisons are generated by models under explicit principles.

The pipeline is easiest to understand as a change in where supervision enters. The constitution supplies a visible rule, the critique–revision loop turns that rule into examples, and the preference model turns it into a scalar signal for RL. The supervised stage also gives RL a less harmful starting distribution, so the policy does not have to discover the safe region while optimizing the preference model.

The main evaluation asks whether this cheaper supervision changes the helpfulness–harmlessness frontier. In the 52B comparison, crowdworkers prefer RL-CAI behavior to the earlier human-feedback baselines at comparable helpfulness, and the model is less evasive than a policy that simply refuses harmful prompts. That result is stronger than a preference-model score alone because the final judgment is still external to the AI feedback loop.

What does the constitution itself contribute? Figure 6 separates the number of principles from the number of revisions. More principles do not materially raise the harmlessness preference-model score, while repeated revisions raise the score across the plotted settings. The authors argue that a larger constitution can still diversify revisions and improve later exploration, but they do not quantify that diversity in this experiment. The plotted evidence supports the score comparison; the proposed diversity benefit remains an unquantified observation from the authors.

Figure 2 tests the final trade-off directly. The human-feedback Helpful and HH runs move along a less favorable helpfulness–harmlessness frontier, while the RL-CAI traces move upward at similar horizontal positions. The comparison is based on crowdworker Elo, and later RL snapshots move along each curve. It is evidence about the trained assistant's behavior, not proof that the constitution is a complete safety specification: the feedback model can still misread a principle, and the preference-model score becomes less calibrated at high values.

### What the constitution still delegates to evaluation

Constitutional AI replaces repeated harmfulness labels with a legible rule-to-example pipeline, but it does not make the evaluator self-validating. The paper establishes the method on dialogue harmlessness, with crowdworker evaluation as the final check. The unresolved object is whether the same principle can remain correctly interpreted when the behavior being judged is harder to observe than a response. The paper's own evidence says where to look: revision count changes the preference-model score, principle count changes the diversity hypothesis, and the final frontier still needs an independent evaluator.

## High-Level Takeaways

- The atomic supervision units are a critique–revision example and a constitutional preference pair; human harmfulness labels are replaced by model judgments while helpfulness supervision remains human in the reported setup.
- Revisions improve the preference-model score, but adding principles does not; the proposed value of a larger constitution is more diverse exploration, which the paper does not quantify directly.
- RL-CAI improves the crowdworker helpfulness–harmlessness frontier for the reported 52B runs, while preference-model scores alone are less trustworthy at high values.
- A constitution is a specification and data generator, not an independent safety oracle; later work must test whether the feedback model interprets each principle correctly under new behavior and distributions.
