## 2017 – Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms (PPO)

**arXiv:** [1707.06347](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)

**Code:** [openai/baselines](https://github.com/openai/baselines), [OpenAI Spinning Up](https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/ppo.html)

PPO asks how far an on-policy actor can reuse a rollout before the data become misleading. Vanilla policy gradients take one noisy step. TRPO constrains the update with a second-order KL trust region. PPO keeps ordinary first-order optimization and makes overly large probability-ratio changes stop improving the surrogate objective.

## Paper Insights

![PPO's clipped surrogate flattens reward-improving probability-ratio changes outside the epsilon interval](/assets/images/proximal-policy-optimization-ppo-paper-figure.png)
_For positive and negative advantages, clipping removes the incentive to move the action probability beyond the trusted band in the apparently helpful direction. It does not impose a hard KL constraint. Source: [PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)._

With ratio $r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)/\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t\mid s_t)$ and estimated advantage $\hat A_t$, PPO maximizes

$$
L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_t\left[
\min\left(
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t,
\operatorname{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_t
\right)
\right].
$$

The minimum creates a pessimistic lower surrogate. If an action has positive advantage, increasing its probability above $1+\epsilon$ stops helping; if it has negative advantage, decreasing its probability below $1-\epsilon$ stops helping. Movement in the harmful direction is not clipped away. This asymmetry is what makes the objective conservative rather than merely capped.

The full algorithm alternates rollout collection with several minibatch epochs over those same samples, usually combining the clipped actor loss with a value-function loss and entropy bonus. Generalized Advantage Estimation supplies lower-variance advantages. PPO is still on-policy: after several epochs, it must discard the batch and sample from the updated policy.

| Source-paper comparison | Reported conclusion | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous-control benchmark suite | Clipped PPO performs best among the tested surrogate variants on the normalized aggregate | Aggregate includes task and seed variation |
| PPO versus TRPO | PPO matches or exceeds the trust-region baseline across most tested tasks | Not a proof of a true trust region |
| Atari | Better sample complexity than synchronous A2C and comparable overall results to ACER | Online environment interaction remains expensive |
| Clipping versus adaptive KL penalty | Clipping is simpler and stronger in the reported sweep | Later domains can prefer explicit KL control |

The paper's lasting contribution is operational. One can implement the core actor loss compactly and optimize it with Adam:

```python
def ppo_clip_loss(policy, old_logp, obs, act, adv, clip_eps=0.2):
    logp = policy.log_prob(obs, act)
    ratio = torch.exp(logp - old_logp)
    unclipped = ratio * adv
    clipped = torch.clamp(ratio, 1 - clip_eps, 1 + clip_eps) * adv
    return -torch.mean(torch.min(unclipped, clipped))
```

The playground makes the update boundary concrete: vary the old and new log-probabilities, advantage sign, and clipping threshold to see which direction becomes flat.

## Decision Lens

PPO informs whether policy stability requires solving a constrained second-order problem. Its atomic unit is an on-policy transition with an advantage estimate, reused for several first-order epochs. It is attractive when environment interaction is available, action likelihood is tractable, and a value function can learn enough structure to reduce variance.

Clipping is a heuristic, not a guarantee that the whole policy remains close in KL. Results depend on reward scaling, advantage normalization, rollout horizon, epoch count, value loss, entropy, and distributed freshness. At ten times policy size, rollout generation and keeping actors synchronized dominate the inexpensive-looking objective.

[GRPO](/paper%20shorts/2024/02/05/deepseekmath-group-relative-policy-optimization-grpo.html) retains PPO-style ratios and clipping but removes the value model by comparing same-prompt completions. [DPO](/paper%20shorts/2023/05/01/direct-preference-optimization-dpo.html) is a separate offline branch: it removes online rollouts and advantages entirely.

**Context:** PPO turns trust-region intuition into a first-order clipped surrogate that can reuse fresh rollout data for multiple updates.

**Limits:** It remains sample-hungry and sensitive to implementation details; clipping neither validates rewards nor guarantees a bounded policy KL.

**Takeaway:** PPO became the default policy optimizer because it makes conservative online updates practical, not because it solves policy stability once and for all.
